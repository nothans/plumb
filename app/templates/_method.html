Probabilities ("beats next", track leads, prize confidence) also account for how alike the projects are: when the true differences between projects are small next to the noise, a gap between two scores is mostly noise, so the probability stays near even. They are averaged over everything the reviews leave uncertain (how far apart projects truly are, how much judges lean, how noisy a review is), which is what keeps them honest on small events. Intervals are 90% intervals from Student's t{% if fit.dof %} with {{ fit.dof }} degrees of freedom{% endif %}.

Each review's criterion scores are combined with the rubric weights into one number from 0 to 100. Then every score is modelled as project quality + judge lean + noise, and each judge's lean is estimated from the projects they share with other judges. A judge who reviewed few projects is assumed to be typical unless the data says otherwise. The adjusted score is the project's quality with judge lean removed; it is not pulled toward the middle for having fewer reviews, but its interval is wider.
