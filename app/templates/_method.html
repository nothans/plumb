Probabilities ("beats next", track leads, prize confidence) also account for how alike the projects are: when the true differences between projects are small next to the noise, a gap between two scores is mostly noise, so the probability stays near 50%. That is what makes them trustworthy: across simulated events, a stated 70% comes true about 70% of the time.

Each review's criterion scores are combined with the rubric weights into one number from 0 to 100. Then every score is modelled as project quality + judge lean + noise, and each judge's lean is estimated from the projects they share with other judges. A judge who reviewed few projects is assumed to be typical unless the data says otherwise. The adjusted score is the project's quality with judge lean removed; it is not pulled toward the middle for having fewer reviews, but its interval is wider.
